## 🤔 Why I Build Software 💭

Ever since I was a child, I have been fascinated by complex systems — especially mechanical, electrical, and electronic devices — and driven by a curiosity to understand how they work and how they could be improved.  

While many of my friends enjoyed playing with their toys, I was often more interested in taking things apart, studying the components, and figuring out what made them function.  

When older devices became obsolete, I would look for ways to combine their parts and give them a new purpose rather than simply throwing them away.  

That same curiosity eventually led me to computers and to the remarkable idea that, through software, we can give instructions to a machine and enable it to perform complex operations at extraordinary speed and accuracy. It continues to motivate me every day.  

From managing computer systems and networks early in my working life to later pursuing software development, I have always been drawn to understanding technology and using it to solve real-world problems. I enjoy taking a complex idea, breaking it into smaller challenges, and gradually turning it into something functional, useful, and well designed.  

Building software brings together many of the things that motivate me most: problem-solving, creativity, continuous learning, and the satisfaction of creating something tangible from an idea.  

I am particularly interested in building intelligent, practical software that helps people and businesses work more efficiently, which is why I continue to explore AI and machine learning.  

My long-term goal is to grow into an AI/ML engineer or architect and contribute to systems that make advanced technology more useful, accessible, and meaningful.  

For me, software development is not only about writing code — it is about understanding complex problems, imagining better solutions, and turning those ideas into reality. Today, I am building web applications. Tomorrow, I aim to be part of the teams developing the technology that helps solve humanity’s biggest challenges.
